1. Letter to shareholders

Dear shareholders,

2024 was a year of strong progress for Kestrel Semiconductor. Revenue reached USD 2.2 billion, up 8.4% on 2023, and our operating margin was 27.9%. I want to thank our employees, customers and partners for their commitment throughout the year.

Our Automotive Chips segment was the main driver of growth, with revenue up 15.8%. We continued to invest in our chips and in the digital tools our customers use every day, while keeping a close eye on costs. We simplified our organisation and moved more of our support functions to shared service centres.

Our strategy remains unchanged. Kestrel Semiconductor Ltd. designs and manufactures power-management and connectivity chips for automotive suppliers, server makers and consumer-electronics brands. We want to be the partner of choice in each of our markets, to grow faster than our industry and to convert a high share of our earnings into cash. Capital discipline remains central to how we allocate resources.

Looking ahead, we see both opportunities and risks. Demand in some of our end markets remains uncertain, and inflation in wages and materials has not fully eased. We will stay focused on what we can control: serving our customers well, improving productivity and developing our people.

Sincerely,
Isabel Xu
Chief Executive Officer

2. Business overview

Kestrel Semiconductor Ltd. was founded in 1966 and designs and manufactures power-management and connectivity chips. We serve automotive suppliers, server makers and consumer-electronics brands in 55 countries. Our headquarters are in Hsinchu.

We report three segments: Automotive Chips, Data Center and Consumer Devices.

Automotive Chips. The Automotive Chips segment accounted for about 27% of revenue in 2024. Its revenue is partly seasonal, with a stronger second half.

Data Center. The Data Center segment accounted for about 37% of revenue in 2024. Its revenue is partly seasonal, with a stronger second half.

Consumer Devices. The Consumer Devices segment accounted for about 36% of revenue in 2024. It competes mainly on quality, reliability and service.

Europe, Middle East and Africa is our largest region. We also have operations in Americas and Asia Pacific.

At 31 December 2024, we employed approximately 4,400 people.

3. Risk factors

The risks below could materially affect our business, financial condition and results. They are not the only risks we face.

Climate and physical risks

Extreme weather can damage our facilities and those of our suppliers and customers. New climate regulation may increase our energy and compliance costs.

Taxation

We operate in many tax jurisdictions. Changes in tax laws, including the global minimum tax, or disputes with tax authorities could increase our effective tax rate.

Export controls

Export-control rules may restrict sales of some of our products to certain customers or countries, or the use of some equipment in our fabs.

Acquisitions and integration

We have made, and may continue to make, acquisitions. We may not integrate acquired businesses successfully or realise the expected synergies, and we may need to record impairments of goodwill.

Interest rates and financing

We borrow under bank facilities and bonds. Higher interest rates increase our finance costs, and a deterioration of credit markets could make it harder to refinance our borrowings when they mature.

Talent

Our success depends on attracting and retaining skilled employees, especially engineers and experienced managers. Competition for talent is intense in all our main markets.

Macroeconomic conditions

A slowdown in the economies where we operate, high inflation or a recession could reduce demand for our chips and put pressure on our prices. We may not be able to reduce our costs as quickly as our revenue falls.

Industry cycles

The semiconductor industry is cyclical. In a downturn, customers reduce orders and draw down their inventories, and our fabs run below capacity.

4. Management's discussion and analysis

Overview

The following discussion compares our results for 2024 with 2023. All amounts are in USD millions unless stated otherwise, and percentages are calculated on unrounded figures.

Revenue

Revenue increased 8.4% to USD 2,175.8 million in 2024 from USD 2,007.6 million in 2023. Automotive Chips revenue grew 15.8%. Data Center revenue grew 4.9%. Consumer Devices revenue grew 6.8%.

Gross margin and operating expenses

Gross margin was 52.4%, compared with 53.6% in 2023. Research and development expenses were 15.6% of revenue. Selling, general and administrative expenses were 8.9% of revenue.

Operating income and net income

Operating income was USD 607.6 million, and the operating margin was 27.9% (29.8% in 2023). Net income was USD 460.1 million and diluted earnings per share were USD 4.00.

Liquidity and capital resources

Net cash from operating activities was USD 674.0 million. We invested USD 435.9 million in property, plant and equipment and paid dividends of USD 149.7 million. At year end, cash and cash equivalents were USD 623.2 million and total borrowings were USD 825.6 million.

Outlook

We expect market conditions in 2025 to remain mixed. We plan to keep investing in our priority segments while managing costs carefully. Our guidance for the coming year is published separately with our fourth-quarter results.
