1. Letter to shareholders

Dear shareholders,

2025 was a year of strong progress for Northgate Payments. Revenue reached USD 1.3 billion, up 15.0% on 2024, and our operating margin was 21.0%. I want to thank our employees, customers and partners for their commitment throughout the year.

Our Merchant Acquiring segment was the main driver of growth, with revenue up 17.0%. We continued to invest in our payment volume and in the digital tools our customers use every day, while keeping a close eye on costs. We opened new facilities and expanded our sales teams in several markets.

Our strategy remains unchanged. Northgate Payments Corp. processes card and account-to-account payments for merchants, banks and marketplaces for retailers, online marketplaces and regional banks. We want to be the partner of choice in each of our markets, to grow faster than our industry and to convert a high share of our earnings into cash. We will keep returning surplus cash to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases.

Looking ahead, we see both opportunities and risks. Demand in some of our end markets remains uncertain, and inflation in wages and materials has not fully eased. We will stay focused on what we can control: serving our customers well, improving productivity and developing our people.

Sincerely,
Leon Nyberg
Chief Executive Officer

2. Business overview

Northgate Payments Corp. was founded in 1974 and processes card and account-to-account payments for merchants, banks and marketplaces. We serve retailers, online marketplaces and regional banks in 48 countries. Our headquarters are in Charlotte.

We report three segments: Merchant Acquiring, Issuer Processing and Cross-Border Payments.

Merchant Acquiring. The Merchant Acquiring segment accounted for about 41% of revenue in 2025. Its customers typically sign multi-year agreements.

Issuer Processing. The Issuer Processing segment accounted for about 26% of revenue in 2025. Its revenue is partly seasonal, with a stronger second half.

Cross-Border Payments. The Cross-Border Payments segment accounted for about 33% of revenue in 2025. Its revenue is partly seasonal, with a stronger second half.

Asia Pacific is our largest region. We also have operations in Americas and Europe, Middle East and Africa.

At 31 December 2025, we employed approximately 3,000 people.

3. Risk factors

The risks below could materially affect our business, financial condition and results. They are not the only risks we face.

Foreign exchange

We report in US dollars, but a significant part of our revenue and costs is in other currencies. Exchange-rate movements affect our reported results and the value of our foreign assets.

Regulation and compliance

We are subject to many laws, including competition, anti-corruption, sanctions, tax and data-protection rules. Changes in these laws, or a failure to comply with them, could result in penalties and restrictions on our business.

Litigation

From time to time we are involved in legal proceedings, including commercial disputes and employment claims. An unfavourable outcome could require us to pay damages or change how we operate.

Payment network rules and fees

We rely on card networks and real-time payment schemes. Changes to network rules, interchange caps or scheme fees could reduce the margin we earn on each transaction.

Fraud and chargebacks

We bear losses when merchants cannot cover chargebacks. A rise in card-not-present fraud would increase our transaction losses and could damage our relationships with banks.

Climate and physical risks

Extreme weather can damage our facilities and those of our suppliers and customers. New climate regulation may increase our energy and compliance costs.

Acquisitions and integration

We have made, and may continue to make, acquisitions. We may not integrate acquired businesses successfully or realise the expected synergies, and we may need to record impairments of goodwill.

Interest rates and financing

We borrow under bank facilities and bonds. Higher interest rates increase our finance costs, and a deterioration of credit markets could make it harder to refinance our borrowings when they mature.

4. Management's discussion and analysis

Overview

The following discussion compares our results for 2025 with 2024. All amounts are in USD millions unless stated otherwise, and percentages are calculated on unrounded figures.

Revenue

Revenue increased 15.0% to USD 1,333.6 million in 2025 from USD 1,159.9 million in 2024. Merchant Acquiring revenue grew 17.0%. Issuer Processing revenue grew 10.9%. Cross-Border Payments revenue grew 15.7%.

Gross margin and operating expenses

Gross margin was 55.4%, compared with 56.0% in 2024. Research and development expenses were 11.1% of revenue. Selling, general and administrative expenses were 23.3% of revenue.

Operating income and net income

Operating income was USD 280.5 million, and the operating margin was 21.0% (20.4% in 2024). Net income was USD 191.0 million and diluted earnings per share were USD 2.19.

Liquidity and capital resources

Net cash from operating activities was USD 223.7 million. We invested USD 43.0 million in property, plant and equipment and paid dividends of USD 50.1 million. At year end, cash and cash equivalents were USD 112.5 million and total borrowings were USD 484.6 million.

Outlook

We expect market conditions in 2026 to remain mixed. We plan to keep investing in our priority segments while managing costs carefully. Our guidance for the coming year is published separately with our fourth-quarter results.
