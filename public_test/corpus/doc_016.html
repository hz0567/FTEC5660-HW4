1. Letter to shareholders

Dear shareholders,

2025 was a year of steady progress for Halcyon Freight. Revenue reached USD 1.9 billion, up 2.6% on 2024, and our operating margin was 13.9%. I want to thank our employees, customers and partners for their commitment throughout the year.

Our Intermodal segment was the main driver of growth, with revenue up 10.7%. We continued to invest in our shipments and in the digital tools our customers use every day, while keeping a close eye on costs. We opened new facilities and expanded our sales teams in several markets.

Our strategy remains unchanged. Halcyon Freight Inc. moves freight by road and rail and runs warehouses on behalf of customers for retailers, manufacturers and e-commerce platforms. We want to be the partner of choice in each of our markets, to grow faster than our industry and to convert a high share of our earnings into cash. We will keep returning surplus cash to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases.

Looking ahead, we see both opportunities and risks. Demand in some of our end markets remains uncertain, and inflation in wages and materials has not fully eased. We will stay focused on what we can control: serving our customers well, improving productivity and developing our people.

Sincerely,
James Kaur
Chief Executive Officer

2. Business overview

Halcyon Freight Inc. was founded in 1985 and moves freight by road and rail and runs warehouses on behalf of customers. We serve retailers, manufacturers and e-commerce platforms in 59 countries. Our headquarters are in Memphis.

We report three segments: Truckload, Intermodal and Contract Logistics.

Truckload. The Truckload segment accounted for about 44% of revenue in 2025. Its revenue is partly seasonal, with a stronger second half.

Intermodal. The Intermodal segment accounted for about 31% of revenue in 2025. It competes mainly on quality, reliability and service.

Contract Logistics. The Contract Logistics segment accounted for about 25% of revenue in 2025. It competes mainly on quality, reliability and service.

Asia Pacific is our largest region. We also have operations in Americas and Europe, Middle East and Africa.

At 31 December 2025, we employed approximately 9,400 people.

3. Risk factors

The risks below could materially affect our business, financial condition and results. They are not the only risks we face.

Macroeconomic conditions

A slowdown in the economies where we operate, high inflation or a recession could reduce demand for our shipments and put pressure on our prices. We may not be able to reduce our costs as quickly as our revenue falls.

Talent

Our success depends on attracting and retaining skilled employees, especially engineers and experienced managers. Competition for talent is intense in all our main markets.

Cybersecurity and data protection

Our systems and those of our partners are targets of cyberattacks. A successful attack could disrupt our operations, expose personal data and lead to regulatory investigations and claims.

Foreign exchange

We report in US dollars, but a significant part of our revenue and costs is in other currencies. Exchange-rate movements affect our reported results and the value of our foreign assets.

Climate and physical risks

Extreme weather can damage our facilities and those of our suppliers and customers. New climate regulation may increase our energy and compliance costs.

Driver availability

The industry faces a shortage of qualified truck drivers. Higher wages and recruiting costs may reduce our margins, and a lack of drivers may limit our capacity.

Fuel prices

Fuel is one of our largest operating costs. Fuel surcharges recover most, but not all, of an increase in diesel prices, and they recover it with a delay.

Regulation and compliance

We are subject to many laws, including competition, anti-corruption, sanctions, tax and data-protection rules. Changes in these laws, or a failure to comply with them, could result in penalties and restrictions on our business.

4. Management's discussion and analysis

Overview

The following discussion compares our results for 2025 with 2024. All amounts are in USD millions unless stated otherwise, and percentages are calculated on unrounded figures.

Revenue

Revenue increased 2.6% to USD 1,911.3 million in 2025 from USD 1,862.8 million in 2024. Truckload revenue fell 2.6%. Intermodal revenue grew 10.7%. Contract Logistics revenue grew 3.1%.

Gross margin and operating expenses

Gross margin was 24.7%, compared with 24.1% in 2024. Selling, general and administrative expenses were 10.8% of revenue.

Operating income and net income

Operating income was USD 266.4 million, and the operating margin was 13.9% (13.9% in 2024). Net income was USD 186.6 million and diluted earnings per share were USD 6.39.

Liquidity and capital resources

Net cash from operating activities was USD 302.8 million. We invested USD 217.2 million in property, plant and equipment and paid dividends of USD 74.4 million. At year end, cash and cash equivalents were USD 332.8 million and total borrowings were USD 585.2 million.

Outlook

We expect market conditions in 2026 to remain mixed. We plan to keep investing in our priority segments while managing costs carefully. Our guidance for the coming year is published separately with our fourth-quarter results.
