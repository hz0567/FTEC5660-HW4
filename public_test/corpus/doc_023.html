1. Letter to shareholders

Dear shareholders,

2025 was a challenging year for Lumen Harbor Foods. Revenue reached USD 1.8 billion, up 1.6% on 2024, and our operating margin was 14.9%. I want to thank our employees, customers and partners for their commitment throughout the year.

Our Bakery segment was the main driver of growth, with revenue up 5.8%. We continued to invest in our products and in the digital tools our customers use every day, while keeping a close eye on costs. We simplified our organisation and moved more of our support functions to shared service centres.

Our strategy remains unchanged. Lumen Harbor Foods plc produces and sells branded and private-label packaged foods for supermarket chains, convenience stores and food-service distributors. We want to be the partner of choice in each of our markets, to grow faster than our industry and to convert a high share of our earnings into cash. Capital discipline remains central to how we allocate resources.

Looking ahead, we see both opportunities and risks. Demand in some of our end markets remains uncertain, and inflation in wages and materials has not fully eased. We will stay focused on what we can control: serving our customers well, improving productivity and developing our people.

Sincerely,
Qing Abbott
Chief Executive Officer

2. Business overview

Lumen Harbor Foods plc was founded in 1961 and produces and sells branded and private-label packaged foods. We serve supermarket chains, convenience stores and food-service distributors in 51 countries. Our headquarters are in Manchester.

We report three segments: Bakery, Dairy and Chilled and Snacks.

Bakery. The Bakery segment accounted for about 36% of revenue in 2025. It competes mainly on quality, reliability and service.

Dairy and Chilled. The Dairy and Chilled segment accounted for about 31% of revenue in 2025. Its customers typically sign multi-year agreements.

Snacks. The Snacks segment accounted for about 33% of revenue in 2025. It has been our fastest-growing business over the last three years.

Asia Pacific is our largest region. We also have operations in Americas and Europe, Middle East and Africa.

At 31 December 2025, we employed approximately 8,300 people.

3. Risk factors

The risks below could materially affect our business, financial condition and results. They are not the only risks we face.

Acquisitions and integration

We have made, and may continue to make, acquisitions. We may not integrate acquired businesses successfully or realise the expected synergies, and we may need to record impairments of goodwill.

Interest rates and financing

We borrow under bank facilities and bonds. Higher interest rates increase our finance costs, and a deterioration of credit markets could make it harder to refinance our borrowings when they mature.

Commodity prices

Wheat, dairy, sugar and packaging account for most of our cost of revenue. We cannot always pass sudden increases in commodity prices on to retailers.

Macroeconomic conditions

A slowdown in the economies where we operate, high inflation or a recession could reduce demand for our products and put pressure on our prices. We may not be able to reduce our costs as quickly as our revenue falls.

Taxation

We operate in many tax jurisdictions. Changes in tax laws, including the global minimum tax, or disputes with tax authorities could increase our effective tax rate.

Cybersecurity and data protection

Our systems and those of our partners are targets of cyberattacks. A successful attack could disrupt our operations, expose personal data and lead to regulatory investigations and claims.

Climate and physical risks

Extreme weather can damage our facilities and those of our suppliers and customers. New climate regulation may increase our energy and compliance costs.

Retailer concentration

A few supermarket chains account for a large part of our sales, and they can negotiate lower prices, longer payment terms or more shelf-space fees.

4. Management's discussion and analysis

Overview

The following discussion compares our results for 2025 with 2024. All amounts are in USD millions unless stated otherwise, and percentages are calculated on unrounded figures.

Revenue

Revenue increased 1.6% to USD 1,848.5 million in 2025 from USD 1,819.4 million in 2024. Bakery revenue grew 5.8%. Dairy and Chilled revenue fell 3.4%. Snacks revenue grew 2.2%.

Gross margin and operating expenses

Gross margin was 31.8%, compared with 33.0% in 2024. Research and development expenses were 1.0% of revenue. Selling, general and administrative expenses were 15.9% of revenue.

Operating income and net income

Operating income was USD 274.7 million, and the operating margin was 14.9% (15.0% in 2024). Net income was USD 200.3 million and diluted earnings per share were USD 4.79.

Liquidity and capital resources

Net cash from operating activities was USD 309.8 million. We invested USD 80.3 million in property, plant and equipment and paid dividends of USD 65.5 million. At year end, cash and cash equivalents were USD 437.4 million and total borrowings were USD 418.5 million.

Outlook

We expect market conditions in 2026 to remain mixed. We plan to keep investing in our priority segments while managing costs carefully. Our guidance for the coming year is published separately with our fourth-quarter results.
