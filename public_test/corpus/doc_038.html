1. Letter to shareholders

Dear shareholders,

2025 was a year of steady progress for Northgate Robotics. Revenue reached USD 2.7 billion, up 3.1% on 2024, and our operating margin was 13.0%. I want to thank our employees, customers and partners for their commitment throughout the year.

Our Collaborative Robots segment was the main driver of growth, with revenue up 7.5%. We continued to invest in our robots and in the digital tools our customers use every day, while keeping a close eye on costs. We opened new facilities and expanded our sales teams in several markets.

Our strategy remains unchanged. Northgate Robotics Inc. designs and manufactures robotic arms, motion controllers and machine-vision systems for automotive, electronics and food-processing manufacturers. We want to be the partner of choice in each of our markets, to grow faster than our industry and to convert a high share of our earnings into cash. Our balance sheet gives us the flexibility to invest through the cycle.

Looking ahead, we see both opportunities and risks. Demand in some of our end markets remains uncertain, and inflation in wages and materials has not fully eased. We will stay focused on what we can control: serving our customers well, improving productivity and developing our people.

Sincerely,
Liam Ferreira
Chief Executive Officer

2. Business overview

Northgate Robotics Inc. was founded in 1981 and designs and manufactures robotic arms, motion controllers and machine-vision systems. We serve automotive, electronics and food-processing manufacturers in 26 countries. Our headquarters are in Pittsburgh.

We report three segments: Factory Automation, Collaborative Robots and Services and Software.

Factory Automation. The Factory Automation segment accounted for about 33% of revenue in 2025. Its customers typically sign multi-year agreements.

Collaborative Robots. The Collaborative Robots segment accounted for about 42% of revenue in 2025. It competes mainly on quality, reliability and service.

Services and Software. The Services and Software segment accounted for about 25% of revenue in 2025. Its customers typically sign multi-year agreements.

Americas is our largest region. We also have operations in Europe, Middle East and Africa and Asia Pacific.

At 31 December 2025, we employed approximately 9,100 people.

3. Risk factors

The risks below could materially affect our business, financial condition and results. They are not the only risks we face.

Taxation

We operate in many tax jurisdictions. Changes in tax laws, including the global minimum tax, or disputes with tax authorities could increase our effective tax rate.

Macroeconomic conditions

A slowdown in the economies where we operate, high inflation or a recession could reduce demand for our robots and put pressure on our prices. We may not be able to reduce our costs as quickly as our revenue falls.

Cyclical demand for capital equipment

Demand for capital equipment is cyclical. When manufacturers postpone factory investments, orders for our robots fall quickly, and our order backlog may not protect revenue for more than two quarters.

Acquisitions and integration

We have made, and may continue to make, acquisitions. We may not integrate acquired businesses successfully or realise the expected synergies, and we may need to record impairments of goodwill.

Talent

Our success depends on attracting and retaining skilled employees, especially engineers and experienced managers. Competition for talent is intense in all our main markets.

Dependence on component suppliers

We depend on a small number of suppliers for precision gearboxes and servo motors. A disruption at one of these suppliers could delay shipments and increase our costs.

Regulation and compliance

We are subject to many laws, including competition, anti-corruption, sanctions, tax and data-protection rules. Changes in these laws, or a failure to comply with them, could result in penalties and restrictions on our business.

Cybersecurity and data protection

Our systems and those of our partners are targets of cyberattacks. A successful attack could disrupt our operations, expose personal data and lead to regulatory investigations and claims.

4. Management's discussion and analysis

Overview

The following discussion compares our results for 2025 with 2024. All amounts are in USD millions unless stated otherwise, and percentages are calculated on unrounded figures.

Revenue

Revenue increased 3.1% to USD 2,710.4 million in 2025 from USD 2,629.1 million in 2024. Factory Automation revenue fell 1.7%. Collaborative Robots revenue grew 7.5%. Services and Software revenue grew 2.6%.

Gross margin and operating expenses

Gross margin was 35.0%, compared with 34.5% in 2024. Research and development expenses were 6.7% of revenue. Selling, general and administrative expenses were 15.2% of revenue.

Operating income and net income

Operating income was USD 353.1 million, and the operating margin was 13.0% (11.7% in 2024). Net income was USD 241.6 million and diluted earnings per share were USD 2.25.

Liquidity and capital resources

Net cash from operating activities was USD 342.2 million. We invested USD 172.3 million in property, plant and equipment and paid dividends of USD 52.7 million. At year end, cash and cash equivalents were USD 241.6 million and total borrowings were USD 1,137.8 million.

Outlook

We expect market conditions in 2026 to remain mixed. We plan to keep investing in our priority segments while managing costs carefully. Our guidance for the coming year is published separately with our fourth-quarter results.
